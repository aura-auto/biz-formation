The short version

TL;DR: doola costs $297/year for formation, EIN, registered agent, and a US address -- but the subscription model, upsells past the Starter tier, and mixed BBB reputation push many founders to look elsewhere. For non-US founders who want the same bundle at a lower long-term cost, Firstbase charges $399 one-time for formation plus EIN, with registered agent available as a $299/year add-on. If you only need the cheapest possible bare-bones filing and can handle EIN yourself, Northwest Registered Agent starts at $39 plus state fees with free registered agent for year one. See Firstbase pricing.

Why are people looking for doola alternatives?

doola launched in 2020 as StartPack, rebranded, and carved out a strong position with non-US founders who need a US LLC without a Social Security Number. It is backed by Y Combinator, claims 15,000+ founders across 175+ countries, and holds a 4.0 to 4.7 Trustpilot rating from over 2,000 reviews. That sounds solid -- until you look at the Better Business Bureau side.

doola carries a 1-star, non-accredited BBB rating, with complaints concentrated around billing disputes, refund handling, and shared business addresses flagged as scam-looking when searched online. Several Reddit threads from r/SaaS and r/Entrepreneur mention quality deteriorating after payment, long wait times for formation, and unclear refund policies. The Trustpilot and BBB split is genuinely unusual -- most formation services have a consistent reputation across platforms.

Beyond reputation, the subscription model is the other friction point. doola's Starter plan at $297/year covers formation, EIN, registered agent, and a US address. That is not expensive by itself. But the deeper services -- bookkeeping at $300/year, tax filing at $1,999/year, and the full "Business-in-a-Box" at $2,999/year -- add up fast for founders who expected one price to cover everything. The registered agent renewal after year one is also unclear in some reviews, and there is no clear public price for it outside the bundles.

If you have been burned by doola's upsells, slow support, or just want a simpler path, here are four alternatives worth comparing.

Which doola alternative is best for non-US founders?

When to pick Firstbase: You want formation plus EIN bundled at a one-time price, and you do not mind paying separately for registered agent and mail service later. Strong for C-Corp formations if you plan to raise VC.

Avoid if: You want registered agent included in the base price, or you need ongoing bookkeeping and tax filing from a single provider.
Cost: Start plan (formation + EIN) is $399 one-time. Registered Agent is $299/year. Mailroom Premium is $350/year. Firstbase One bundle is $199/month billed yearly ($2,388/year). See Firstbase plans.

Firstbase is the closest structural alternative to doola for non-US founders. Both handle EIN applications without an SSN -- the step that trips up most international entrepreneurs. The core difference is pricing: Firstbase charges $399 once instead of $297 every year. Over three years, Firstbase costs about $399 + $897 in registered agent fees ($299 x 3) = $1,296. doola's Starter over three years is $891 plus whatever registered agent renewal costs after year one.

Firstbase also includes all required corporate documents (stock purchase agreement, bylaws, etc.) and gives you visibility into compliance dashboards. The trade-off is that Firstbase's add-ons -- mailroom, agent autopilot, accounting, and tax filing -- are priced separately and can add up if you need them all. For founders who want formation and EIN as a one-time purchase and will figure out the rest on their own, Firstbase is the cleaner deal.

Is Northwest cheaper than doola for US founders?

When to pick Northwest: You are a US-based founder with an SSN who wants the absolute cheapest formation with privacy protections. Northwest does not sell customer data, and their formation includes one year of free registered agent service.

Avoid if: You are a non-US founder without an SSN -- the $200 EIN add-on changes the math, and you do not get the guided onboarding doola provides.
Cost: $39 flat formation fee plus state filing. Year-one registered agent free. Year two and beyond: $125/year. EIN for non-SSN holders: $200. See Northwest pricing.

Northwest is the transparency pick. No subscription tiers, no hidden upsells at checkout, no plan upgrades pushed during onboarding. You pay $39, you get formation, a free year of registered agent, an operating agreement template, and Northwest's own address on your state filings instead of your home address. The Business Identity bundle thrown in covers a domain, website hosting, email, phone service, and mail scanning.

The catch for non-US founders is the EIN. Northwest charges $200 to handle the EIN application for someone without an SSN -- pushing the real year-one cost to roughly $239. Still cheaper than doola, but not by the gap the $39 headline suggests. For US founders who already have an SSN and can get an EIN through the IRS online tool for free, Northwest at $39 is hard to beat.

Can ZenBusiness replace doola on a tight budget?

When to pick ZenBusiness: You want the cheapest possible formation and plan to add services (registered agent, compliance, bookkeeping) as you grow, not all at once. ZenBusiness also has a free Velo AI tool that guides you through the process.

Avoid if: You are a non-US founder who needs EIN help, US banking setup, or a US business address -- ZenBusiness is built for the domestic market.
Cost: Starter $0 + state fees, Pro $199/year, Premium $399/year. Registered agent $199/year. EIN add-on $99. See ZenBusiness pricing.

ZenBusiness matches doola on one front: it also starts at effectively $0 for formation. The difference is that doola bundles EIN, registered agent, and US address into that $297, while ZenBusiness sells each separately. Registered agent is $199/year. EIN is $99 one-time. An operating agreement template is $99.

For a US-based founder watching every dollar, ZenBusiness is the leanest entry. For international founders, it lacks the cross-border support that doola and Firstbase were built for. No US address, no Mercury banking connection, no EIN filing for non-residents out of the box.

Should you just use LegalZoom for formation?

When to pick LegalZoom: You want the biggest name in online legal services, access to an attorney network, and are comfortable paying premium prices for that brand assurance. Also useful if you need trademark filing or ongoing legal work beyond formation.

Avoid if: You want the cheapest formation, are a non-US founder, or dislike aggressive upsells during checkout -- LegalZoom pushes subscription tiers hard.
Cost: LLC formation $0 + state fees. Registered agent $249/year. Annual compliance plan extra. See LegalZoom LLC plans.

LegalZoom is the name most people recognize, but that brand premium shows up in the price. Registered agent alone runs $249/year -- more than doola's entire Starter plan. The formation itself is $0, but the add-ons stack up fast: compliance plans, attorney consultations, and the auto-renewing Personal Pro subscription at $9.99/month that gets pushed during checkout.

LegalZoom makes sense if you need ongoing legal services beyond formation -- trademark filings, contract review, attorney consultations. For a straightforward LLC formation, especially as a non-US founder, you are paying more for the name than the service.

Quick recap: which doola alternative should you pick?

  1. Non-US founder, want one-time cost: Firstbase at $399 one-time for formation plus EIN.
  2. US founder, cheapest entry, value privacy: Northwest at $39 plus state fees with free year-one registered agent.
  3. US founder, tightest budget, add services later: ZenBusiness at $0 plus state fees with separate EIN and registered agent add-ons.
  4. Need legal services beyond formation: LegalZoom with its attorney network, at a premium price.

FAQ

Is doola a scam?

No. doola is a legitimate LLC formation service backed by Y Combinator with 15,000+ customers. However, it holds a 1-star, non-accredited BBB rating with complaints about billing and refunds. Its Trustpilot score from 2,000+ reviews is much higher (4.0 to 4.7). The split reputation means experiences vary -- some founders report smooth formation, others report slow support and unclear refund policies.

Can non-US founders use alternatives to doola for EIN filing?

Yes. Firstbase handles EIN applications for non-SSN holders included in its $399 formation price. Northwest charges $200 for the same service. ZenBusiness and LegalZoom both offer EIN add-ons for around $99, but their platforms are designed for US-based founders and do not provide the guided onboarding that international entrepreneurs often need.

How much does doola actually cost per year?

The Starter plan is $297/year and includes formation, EIN, registered agent, operating agreement, and a US business address. Add bookkeeping at $300/year, tax filing at $1,999/year, or the full Business-in-a-Box at $2,999/year. State filing fees ($50 to $110 depending on the state) are extra on all plans.

What happens to my LLC if I cancel doola?

Your LLC remains active -- doola filing the formation does not make doola your LLC's owner. You would need to find a new registered agent (Northwest and ZenBusiness both offer standalone registered agent services) and update your registered agent on file with your state. Failure to maintain a registered agent can result in your LLC losing good-standing status.

Is Northwest really cheaper than doola for non-US founders?

Northwest's $39 headline price is cheaper, but non-US founders need to add $200 for EIN filing, bringing the real cost to roughly $239 -- about $58 less than doola's $297 Starter. The gap narrows when you factor in that doola bundles a US business address and the guided onboarding that Northwest does not offer for international founders. For US-based founders with an SSN, Northwest is clearly cheaper.

Which service is best for forming a C-Corp as a non-US founder?

Firstbase is specifically designed for this use case and includes all required corporate documents (bylaws, stock purchase agreements, board resolutions). doola also supports C-Corps, but at the recurring $297/year rate. Stripe Atlas at $500 one-time is another option if you plan to use Stripe for payments, though it is limited to Delaware and does not support crypto businesses.
