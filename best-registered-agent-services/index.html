What should I actually pay for a registered agent?

Most national services price between $99 and $300 per year. The cheapest first-year deals are Harbor Compliance at $99 and ZenBusiness and Bizee, which both bundle registered agent service into formation packages. On renewal, Northwest's flat $125 is the lowest headline number of the group, followed by Harbor and Bizee at $149.

The gap compounds over time. ZenBusiness renews at $199 and LegalZoom at $249, so a $125 provider saves you roughly $75 to $125 every year after the first one. None of these prices include your state's filing fee, which is handled separately on top.

Northwest Registered Agent: the flat-fee value pick

Northwest keeps things unusually simple. Its official page lists registered agent service at $125 per year with no hidden fees, and the company says it has not raised that price in about 20 years. If you have five or more states to cover, the rate drops to $100 per state per year.

What you get for the flat fee: a business address for service of process, mail scanning, and free identity tools including a domain name, website builder, custom email, and phone line. Northwest is a registered agent company first, not a formation marketplace selling it as a loss leader.

When to pick: You want a predictable flat renewal that will not jump after year one, and you like the idea of free domain and website tools bundled in.
Avoid if: The bundled free tools mean nothing to you and you only need the bare legal function.
Cost: $125 per year, or $100 per state per year at the five-plus-state tier.

Harbor Compliance: cheapest first-year price with pro compliance tools

Harbor Compliance targets owners who want more than a mailbox. New customers pay $99 the first year and renew at $149. Features lean professional: same-day document delivery, Entity Manager software, annual report reminders, and unlimited document storage with no per-delivery fees.

It is also the only provider in this roundup that calls out a SOC 2 Type II certification on its registered agent page, which matters if data handling and security controls are a trigger for you. The $149 renewal sits below ZenBusiness and LegalZoom.

When to pick: You want the lowest first-year cost, a compliance dashboard with entity tracking, and clean renewal pricing.
Avoid if: You want email-only service, or you already have a bundled agent elsewhere.
Cost: $99 for the first year, then $149 per year.

ZenBusiness: cheap entry, higher renewal

ZenBusiness markets registered agent service at $99 for the first year plus your state fee, renewing at $199. Documents get scanned to a secure dashboard with email alerts, and time-sensitive legal notices are mailed and overnighted so you can hit deadlines.

The trade-off is the renewal: at $199 it runs about $50-$74 more per year than Northwest or Harbor after year one. The dashboard and overnight handling can justify it.

When to pick: You want a well-known service with a clean dashboard, email alerts, and fast handling of urgent legal mail.
Avoid if: The lower-priced flat-fee options cover what you need, since $199 renewal is the steepest of the mid-tier group.
Cost: $99 first year plus state fees, $199 per year after.

LegalZoom: the premium bundle

LegalZoom charges $249 per year and auto-renews for scanned documents, email reminders via its Compliance Calendar, unlimited cloud storage, and free help switching agents (state filing fees covered).

That $249 is the highest price here, earning its place through the Compliance Calendar and unlimited storage rather than anything a cheaper service cannot do. Keep the agent there if you already trust LegalZoom, but you are paying a premium for the name.

When to pick: You already use LegalZoom for business services, value the annual-report reminders and unlimited cloud storage, and are fine paying more for one trusted vendor.
Avoid if: Price is your main concern; cheaper services match the core legal function for roughly half as much.
Cost: $249 per year, auto-renewing.

doola and Bizee: free registered agent when you form with them

Both doola and Bizee bundle registered agent service into their formation packages, which changes the math if you have not filed your LLC yet. On doola's official page, registered agent service costs $297 per year on its own but is free when you buy the company's Formation Service. doola's registered agent covers same-day local scans, secure storage for all scanned documents, and annual report notifications.

Bizee (formerly Incfile) offers it free for the first year when you form with it, standalone $149 per year, highlighting scanning, alerts, and annual report help.

When to pick: You are starting from scratch and will form your LLC anyway. Getting a free year of registered agent service softens the formation cost either way.
Avoid if: You already have a registered agent and only need a standalone service, or you prefer a dedicated registered agent provider over a formation brand.
Cost: doola $297 per year standalone, free with Formation Service. Bizee $149 per year standalone, free first year with formation.

How do I pick a registered agent service?

Start with your situation, not the marketing: have you already formed your LLC, and do you operate in more than one state?

Every state you do business in needs its own registered agent, so a flat per-state price like Northwest's ($100 per state at five or more) pulls ahead fast for multi-state operators.

Beyond price, compare the day-to-day function that actually protects you: how fast documents get scanned, whether you get phone or email alerts the moment something urgent arrives, and whether time-sensitive legal notices get overnighted.

Recap checklist before you sign up:

  1. Confirm the renewal price, not just the first-year promo rate, on the provider's own pricing page.
  2. Confirm same-day scanning and whether urgent legal notices get an email alert plus overnight delivery.
  3. If you operate in more than one state, price the per-state cost instead of the single-state headline.
  4. Check whether the registered agent fee is free when bundled with your LLC formation package.
  5. Verify the provider has a physical address in your state of formation so service of process can be accepted.

FAQ

How much does a registered agent cost per year?

Most national services cost between $99 and $300 per year. In this roundup, Harbor Compliance runs $99 for the first year and $149 after, Northwest is a flat $125, ZenBusiness is $99 then $199, Bizee is $149 (free the first year with formation), LegalZoom is $249, and doola is $297.

Do all states require a registered agent?

Effectively yes. Every state requires LLCs and corporations to maintain a registered agent with a physical address in that state, available during business hours to accept legal documents and service of process.

Is a registered agent included in LLC formation packages?

Sometimes. doola includes it free with its Formation Service and Bizee free for the first year; others like LegalZoom and ZenBusiness sell it separately.

Can I be my own registered agent?

Yes, as long as you maintain a physical address in the state, are available during business hours, and are present to accept documents. The downside is privacy, because your name and address go on the public record, and you must be in place to receive service of process.

Why do registered agent prices jump after the first year?

Promo pricing is a common funnel. Northwest avoids it with a flat $125 that has not changed in about 20 years, while providers like ZenBusiness ($199) and LegalZoom ($249) charge notably more at renewal. Always check the renewal rate before signing.
