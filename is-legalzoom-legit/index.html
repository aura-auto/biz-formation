Is LegalZoom a real company?

LegalZoom was founded in 1999 by Brian Liu, Brian Lee, Robert Shapiro, and Eddie Hartman. It went public on Nasdaq in June 2021 (ticker: LZ) at a valuation near $7 billion, then was taken private by Francisco Partners in 2023. The company has been BBB-accredited since November 2001 and has supported over 9 million legal milestones across 25+ years of continuous operation.

So no, it is not a scam. It is a real, publicly traded (then private-equity backed) company with a long track record. The question worth asking is not "is it legit?" but "is it the best deal for what I need?"

What do the review ratings actually show?

LegalZoom's ratings vary a lot depending on where you look, and that spread tells you something important.

On Trustpilot, LegalZoom averages around 4.3 out of 5 stars from over 5,000 reviews. Most positive reviews praise the ease of use and document quality. On SiteJabber/SmartCustomer, the picture is much bleaker: 2.9 out of 5 from 1,785 reviews. On ConsumerAffairs, reviews are mixed, with both glowing 5-star ratings and frustrated 1-star complaints sitting side by side.

Why the gap? Trustpilot skews toward recent, incentivized reviews. SiteJabber captures more organic frustration. The ConsumerAffairs reviews, many of which are verified purchases, give the clearest picture of real customer experience. And the pattern is consistent: the initial formation goes fine, but the billing and cancellation process is where things break down.

What are the most common complaints?

Based on hundreds of verified reviews across SmartCustomer, ConsumerAffairs, and other platforms, the complaints cluster around four areas:

1. Auto-renewal charges after cancellation. This is the single most common complaint. Multiple reviewers report that LegalZoom continued charging them $249/year for registered agent services months or even years after they dissolved their LLC or canceled their service. One reviewer from Florida wrote that LegalZoom charged $249 in 2024 for a registered agent subscription they had canceled in 2022. Another from Texas reported being charged $249 months after their LLC was officially dissolved and the subscription was supposedly canceled. When they called to request a refund, customer service refused.

2. Upselling during the formation process. Several reviewers describe being pushed toward higher-priced tiers and add-on services during checkout. One customer from California noted that what was presented as a straightforward service "quickly became much more expensive due to additional fees and upsells that were not clearly explained upfront." Another reviewer reported their registered agent and service processor fee went from "a couple of hundred dollars to $499 without notice."

3. Slow or inconsistent customer support. Wait times for phone support can stretch past 30 minutes. More problematically, different representatives give different answers to the same question. One reviewer described being "passed between departments without clear solutions." Another said they spent hours on chat and phone calls only to receive conflicting information each time.

4. Lost documents. A few reviewers report LegalZoom losing estate plans. One customer with a 14-year-old plan was told it was gone and would need to be drafted again at additional cost.

Not every review is negative. Many customers report smooth formations and helpful reps. But the billing complaints are persistent enough across platforms to take seriously.

How much does LegalZoom actually cost for an LLC?

LegalZoom's headline prices look affordable. The reality is higher once you factor in what is not included.

Formation tier: Basic is $0 plus your state filing fee. Pro is $249 plus state fee. Premium is $349 plus state fee. State filing fees range from $50 in Kentucky to $500 in Massachusetts, so your total depends on where you form.

Registered agent: Not included in any formation plan. Costs roughly $249/year as an add-on. Most competitors include this for free.

EIN filing: Included in Pro and Premium plans, but not in Basic. If you go with Basic, you can get an EIN directly from the IRS for free, or pay LegalZoom to do it.

Total first-year cost estimate: $400 to $900 depending on your state and which add-ons you choose. That is before any subscription-based legal plans, which start at $49/month.

Compare that to doola's Starter plan at $297/year, which includes LLC formation, EIN, registered agent, and a US business address. For non-US founders especially, the all-in-one approach saves both money and hassle.

When to pick LegalZoom

LegalZoom makes sense if you need legal services beyond LLC formation. Their strength is in estate planning (wills, trusts), intellectual property (trademark searches), and attorney access. The attorney network averages 17 years of experience with flat-fee pricing.

Avoid if: You want straightforward LLC formation at the lowest total cost. The registered agent and EIN add-ons push the price well above competitors. Also avoid if you are a non-US founder who needs a US address, or if auto-renewing subscriptions that require a phone call to cancel bother you.

When to pick doola instead

doola at $297/year is the stronger pick for most first-time founders. The Starter plan bundles formation, EIN, registered agent, and a US business address. For non-US founders, doola also helps with opening a US bank account through their Mercury partnership.

When to pick Northwest instead

Northwest (founded 2001) starts at $39 plus state fee, with free registered agent for the first year. They are known for not selling your data. Good if privacy and low upfront cost matter most. Less ideal for non-US founders who need a bundled US address.

What are the hidden costs of LegalZoom?

The biggest one: registered agent at roughly $249/year. Most competitors include this free. Also watch for auto-renewal subscriptions (canceling requires a phone call), tax preparation fees that can reach $800+, and expedited processing charges on Premium plans.

Should non-US founders use LegalZoom?

Probably not. LegalZoom is built primarily for US-based customers. If you are forming a US LLC from outside the United States, you need a US business address, an EIN, and ideally help opening a US bank account. LegalZoom does not offer a US business address as part of its formation service, which means you would need a separate registered agent service (another $249/year) or a third-party address provider.

doola was designed for this exact use case. Their Starter plan includes all three, and their support team handles international founders every day.
