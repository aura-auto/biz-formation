The short version

TL;DR: For non-US founders forming a US LLC, doola is the best Firstbase alternative. It bundles LLC formation, EIN (even without an SSN), registered agent, and a US business address into a single $297/year subscription. Firstbase charges $399 just for formation, then layers on $299/year for registered agent and $349/year for a mailroom address. Three-year cost with doola: about $891. Three-year cost with Firstbase: about $1,695.

Firstbase has been the default choice for non-US founders forming a US LLC for years. But if you are reading this, something is not working. Here is an honest look at what Firstbase charges, where it falls short, and five alternatives worth considering.

Why founders leave Firstbase

Firstbase Start costs $399 one-time for LLC or C-Corp formation in Delaware or Wyoming. That price includes state filing fees, which is better than most competitors. The formation itself is smooth. The problems start with what the $399 does not include:

  • Registered agent: $299/year. First year is free, but every Delaware or Wyoming LLC must maintain one. At $299/year, that is 2-3x what Northwest ($125/year) or Bizee ($119/year) charge.
  • US business address: $349/year (Mailroom Premium). Non-US founders cannot skip this. You legally need a US address, and Firstbase's premium tier runs $349/year.
  • No EIN-without-SSN handling. Firstbase processes your EIN through their partnership, but non-US founders without a Social Security number report longer timelines.
  • Modular pricing means surprise totals. The $399 headline turns into $748+ in year one once you add the mailroom, then $648/year in year two.

The best Firstbase alternative for non-US founders: doola

doola is the only formation service specifically built for non-US founders. The entire product exists because international entrepreneurs hit a wall: you can file an LLC yourself for $100 in Wyoming, but you cannot get an EIN without a US Social Security number, and you cannot open a Mercury bank account without a US entity and a US address. doola stitches those steps together.

What you get at $297/year: LLC or C-Corp formation in Wyoming or Delaware, EIN application with or without SSN (they handle the Form SS-4 fax process), one year of registered agent service, US business address, Mercury and Relay banking referrals, and an operating agreement. State filing fees are separate: $100 (Wyoming) or $110 (Delaware).

When to pick doola: You want a hands-off setup with EIN, banking, and compliance bundled into one subscription. Avoid if: You are a US resident who can form an LLC for $0-$39 through Bizee or ZenBusiness.

doola's higher tiers: Tax and Compliance at $1,999/year adds Form 5472 filing (mandatory for foreign-owned LLCs, $25,000 penalty if missed). Business-in-a-Box at $2,999/year adds a dedicated bookkeeper.

LegalZoom: best for US residents who want a known brand

LegalZoom is the biggest name in online business formation. Basic plan: $0 + state fees. Pro plan: $249 (includes EIN, operating agreement, attorney consultations). Premium: $299 (adds bookkeeping tools). Registered agent: $199/year.

When to pick LegalZoom: You are a US resident who wants a known brand with legal support and a $0 entry point. Over 4 million businesses formed. Avoid if: You are a non-US founder. LegalZoom does not handle EIN-without-SSN or bundle a US address.

Northwest Registered Agent: cheapest ongoing registered agent

Northwest Registered Agent charges $39 for LLC formation plus $125/year for registered agent service. That registered agent cost is one of the lowest in the industry, and Northwest's core pitch is privacy: they do not sell your data.

What you get: LLC formation: $39 + state fees. Registered agent: $125/year from year one. Corporate guide support with real humans. No bundled US address.

When to pick Northwest: You want the cheapest long-term registered agent cost and care about privacy. Over 5 years, Northwest's RA ($625 total) saves $870 versus Firstbase ($1,495). Avoid if: Non-US founder. No EIN-without-SSN, no US address bundling.

ZenBusiness: best free formation for US founders

ZenBusiness offers free LLC formation on their Starter plan. Pro: $199 (adds operating agreement, EIN). Premium: $299 (adds compliance alerts). Registered agent: $199/year. Over 950,000 businesses launched, A+ BBB rating.

When to pick ZenBusiness: Straightforward US LLC formation with good support and a genuinely free tier. Avoid if: Non-US founder. No EIN-without-SSN or address bundling.

Bizee: cheapest registered agent for US residents

Bizee (formerly Incfile) offers $0 LLC formation with a registered agent at $119/year. Free first year of registered agent with formation.

What you get: Free formation. Registered agent: $119/year (free first year). Gold: $199 (adds operating agreement, EIN). Platinum: $299 (adds domain, website).

When to pick Bizee: Lowest ongoing registered agent cost at $119/year. Avoid if: Non-US founder. No EIN-without-SSN or address bundling.

The honest cost math for non-US founders

If you are a non-US founder, LegalZoom, Northwest, ZenBusiness, and Bizee all have a fatal flaw: they do not bundle the services you actually need. You still have to solve EIN-without-SSN, US address, and banking separately. Hiring a US attorney for those steps runs $1,500-$3,000 per the American Bar Association.

The real comparison for non-US founders is between two services: Firstbase and doola.

  • doola Starter: $297/year. Formation, EIN, registered agent, US address, banking referral. All bundled.
  • Firstbase Start + Agent + Mailroom: $399 (year 1) + $299 (year 2+ RA) + $349/year (address). Year 1: ~$748. Year 2+: ~$648/year.

Over 3 years, doola saves roughly $800. Over 5 years, the savings approach $1,500. The tradeoff: Firstbase supports any of the 50 states while doola focuses on Wyoming and Delaware. For most non-US founders, Wyoming is the right pick anyway (no state income tax, $60 annual report fee).

doola also launched its Formation API in August 2026 (pr-inside.com, August 14, 2026), enabling platforms like Whop to offer native LLC formation through doola's infrastructure.

FAQ

Can I migrate my existing Firstbase LLC to doola?

doola does not transfer existing LLCs from Firstbase. You would need to form a new LLC with doola and dissolve the Firstbase one, or transfer your registered agent to a cheaper provider like Northwest to cut costs while keeping your existing entity.

Does doola handle Form 5472 filing for foreign-owned LLCs?

The Starter plan does not include Form 5472 filing. You need the Tax and Compliance plan at $1,999/year, or hire a CPA separately. Missing Form 5472 carries a $25,000 IRS penalty per year.

Is Wyoming or Delaware better for a non-US founder's LLC?

Wyoming. No state income tax, $60 annual report fee, strong privacy protections, and no requirement to disclose member information publicly. Delaware charges $300/year in franchise tax and is better suited for C-Corps seeking venture funding.

What happens to my registered agent if I leave Firstbase?

You can transfer your registered agent to another provider (Northwest, Bizee, or your new formation service). Firstbase does not lock you in. The transfer typically takes 5-10 business days.

Can I use LegalZoom if I live outside the US?

Technically yes, but it is not designed for you. LegalZoom does not handle EIN-without-SSN, does not bundle a US address, and their support is oriented toward US-based founders. You would need to solve those steps independently.

How does doola's Mercury banking partnership work?

doola refers you to Mercury, a neobank for startups. You still go through Mercury's KYC process, but having a US entity, EIN, and US address from doola satisfies Mercury's minimum requirements. If Mercury rejects you, Relay is also available as an alternative referral.
