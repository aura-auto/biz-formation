The short version

TL;DR: Northwest Registered Agent wins on price, simplicity, and privacy: a flat $39 + state fees for LLC formation, registered agent service free the first year and $125/year after, one plan, and no hard upsell. ZenBusiness wins on features and polish: more free tools (AI guidance, name generator), deeper add-on ecosystem, and far more third-party reviews (4.8/5 from roughly 32,000 on Trustpilot). If you want the cheapest clean LLC and don't mind handling compliance yourself, pick Northwest. If you want an all-in-one dashboard with more hand-holding and don't mind paying for extras, pick ZenBusiness.

Last updated: 2026-08-14. Prices and details verified against official sources.

What does each service actually cost?

Let's get the numbers out first, because they drive most of this decision.

Cost: Northwest charges a flat $39 + your state's filing fee to form an LLC. There is one package, no tiers to pick between. Registered agent service is included free in year one, then renews at $125/year. Add-on EIN filing is $50 one-time.
Cost: ZenBusiness tiers its formation plans: Starter at $0 + state fees, Pro at $199/year, and Premium at $299/year (ZenBusiness pricing). Their registered agent is an add-on at $199/year, Worry-Free Compliance is $199/year, and EIN filing is $99 one-time. If you want the $0 plan plus a registered agent, you're paying $199 a year just for the agent.

State filing fees are separate for both services. Those go directly to your state and you'd pay them regardless of which provider you pick.

What's the three-year picture for each?

This is where the two diverge sharply. Because Northwest bundles the agent into year one and renews it cheaply, its service fees stay low over time.

Northwest, over three years: $39 (year one, agent included) + $125 + $125 = $289 in service fees before state fees.

ZenBusiness Starter, over three years: $0 (year one) + $199 agent + $199 agent = $398, assuming you add the registered agent so you actually have an agent. That's without touching Worry-Free Compliance or any other add-on.

So on raw, recurring fees, Northwest is about a third cheaper out of the gate and stays cheaper every year after.

Who is each service actually for?

When to pick: Northwest, if you're a first-time founder who wants the cheapest legal setup, values your personal address staying out of public records, and is comfortable being pointed at the right form instead of being walked through a software dashboard. Privacy is their whole pitch: they advertise that they list their own address rather than yours and never sell client data. Their "Corporate Guides" are real employees you can call or email.
Avoid if: Northwest, if you want a single dashboard that handles bookkeeping, banking, tax consultations, and website building alongside your LLC, or if you want lots of free-to-use planning tools before you commit. You'll be stitching those pieces together yourself.
When to pick: ZenBusiness, if you want one platform that grows with you, generous free planning tools, and an ecosystem where a registered agent, compliance filings, bookkeeping, and even a website all live under one roof. It's a better fit for founders who'd rather have everything in one place than pay the smallest possible bill.
Avoid if: ZenBusiness, if your only goal is the lowest possible cost and you already know how an LLC works. The $0 Starter plan is tempting, but you'll likely want a registered agent ($199/year) and possibly compliance help, and those add up fast.

What about compliance and registered agent quality?

Both services meet the requirement that your LLC maintain a registered agent to accept legal documents. The practical difference is bundled value: Northwest's registered agent is central to its product, included free in year one and cheap to renew, with mail scanning uploaded to your account the same day. ZenBusiness treats the registered agent as an optional add-on on top of a formation plan.

Neither company prepares annual reports for free. Northwest includes deadline reminders with its agent service; actually filing the report is an extra, optional fee. ZenBusiness sells annual report filing at $100 per report, separate from its Worry-Free Compliance subscription if you want it automated.

Which should a first-time founder choose?

For a basic single-owner LLC in most states, Northwest is the better default. It's cheaper, simpler, has no required annual tier, and keeps your address private. ZenBusiness makes the most sense when you value the platform breadth and don't mind the higher recurring cost, or when you want to take advantage of its heavier third-party track record of reviews before you buy.

Before you decide, run through this checklist:

  1. Look up your state's LLC filing fee and any annual report fee so you can compare real totals, not just the headline number.
  2. Decide whether you want a professional registered agent. If privacy matters and you work from home, you almost certainly do.
  3. Ask whether you'll actually use the extras (website, bookkeeping, tax help) before paying for a platform that bundles them.
  4. Confirm the current annual report filing cost and renewal price for whichever service you lean toward, since these change.
  5. Check each company's current refund and cancellation terms before you order, and note any auto-renewal on annual services.

FAQ

Is Northwest Registered Agent cheaper than ZenBusiness?

Yes, at the base level. Northwest forms an LLC for $39 + state fees with registered agent free the first year, then $125/year. ZenBusiness's free Starter plan needs a $199/year registered agent added on if you want one, so its recurring service fee is higher.

Does ZenBusiness offer a free LLC?

ZenBusiness's Starter plan is $0 plus your state's filing fee. You still pay the state fee, and you'd likely add a registered agent at $199/year, so "free" means free of ZenBusiness's service fee, not free in total.

Which has better reviews, ZenBusiness or Northwest?

By volume and score, ZenBusiness, at roughly 4.8/5 from about 32,000 Trustpilot reviews. Northwest sits around 4.2/5 from a smaller base of about 283 reviews, though its own site reports a higher Google score from 1,826 clients. Review volume reflects reach more than quality.

Do I need a registered agent for my LLC?

Yes. Every state requires an LLC to maintain a registered agent to receive legal and state documents. Most states let you act as your own agent, but then your personal address appears in public records and you must be reachable at it during business hours.

Can I switch registered agents later?

Yes, in most cases. If your needs change, you can change agents and file the appropriate notice with your state. It's one reason to weigh initial price against how the service renews, since the agent fee is the recurring cost that follows you.
