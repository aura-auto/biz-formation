Harbor Compliance Hidden Fees: What You Actually Pay (and When to Pick doola)

2026-09-02 · by Aura Editorial · harbor-compliance registered-agent llc-costs doola

TL;DR: Harbor Compliance is not a scam, but it is not cheap either. Registered agent service is $99 for year one, then $149 a year after that, and everything else (annual report filing, foreign qualification, licenses) is quoted per order plus state fees. If you are a first-time founder forming one LLC, doola Starter at $297/yr bundles formation, EIN, and address in one price and is easier to budget for.

Last updated: 2026-09-02

Harbor Compliance has a solid reputation with mid-size companies and nonprofits. Over 40,000 clients, SOC 2 Type II certified systems, 4.3 out of 5 from 212 Google reviews. That is all stated on their own registered agent service page. So why does this post exist? Because founders searching "harbor compliance hidden fees" are usually staring at a renewal bill or a custom quote that looks nothing like the $99 they signed up for. Here is where the money actually goes.

That table is the whole story. Harbor publishes exactly one number up front. Everything beyond the registered agent is "contact us for a quote." For a compliance company, that is normal. For a founder trying to budget, it is maddening.

Where do Harbor fees surprise buyers?

1. The $99 to $149 renewal jump. Harbor advertises "$99 first year for new customers. Renews at $149 annually" right on the registered agent page, so it is disclosed, not hidden. But plenty of buyers remember the $99 and forget the renewal. Year-two cost is 50% higher. Budget for $149 from the start.

2. Per-state billing stacks fast. Registered agent service is priced per state. One LLC in one state is $99, then $149. Register as a foreign entity in three states and you are paying that fee three times over, plus each state's foreign qualification fee. Harbor's own page notes competitors average $250 to $350 per state with large annual increases, which makes Harbor look cheap. It is cheaper per state, but multi-state founders still get a surprise at checkout.

3. Annual report filing has no published flat price. Harbor offers annual report service, but there is no pricing page with a dollar figure. You go through their order wizard or talk to sales, and the quote adds Harbor's service fee on top of the state filing fee. Compare that with states themselves: most annual report fees run $20 to $300, and filing takes 15 minutes on the Secretary of State site. If all you need is one annual report in one state, paying a service fee on top may not be worth it.

4. Change-of-agent state fees are extra. Switching your registered agent to Harbor requires filing a change-of-agent form with the state, and the state charges its own fee ($10 to $100+ depending on the state). Harbor says it will handle the process if you get in touch, but the state fee is still yours. Small, but it is the kind of line item nobody warns you about.

5. Formation is not the product. Harbor sells ongoing compliance, Entity Manager software, license tracking, multi-state monitoring. If you just need an LLC formed, you are buying a forklift to carry a grocery bag. A formation-focused service bundles the filing, EIN, and operating agreement from a single published price. Harbor quotes it as part of a larger engagement.

When is Harbor actually worth it?

Be fair: Harbor earns its keep for the right buyer. If you run a nonprofit registering to fundraise in a dozen states, a construction firm tracking contractor licenses across jurisdictions, or a company with five entities and staff turnover, Harbor's Entity Manager software and direct Secretary of State integrations are genuinely useful. Same-day document delivery with a phone call after service of process is above-average service. SOC 2 Type II certification matters if you handle sensitive data. For complex, multi-state compliance, Harbor's per-order pricing buys real expertise.

When should you pick doola instead?

When to pick doola: you are forming your first LLC or C Corp, you want one published price, and you want formation, EIN, and a US business address handled without sales calls. doola Starter is $297/yr, and the pricing page lists every tier in plain numbers: Pulse bookkeeping $300/yr, Tax and Compliance $1,999/yr, Business-in-a-Box $2,999/yr. No quote required to know what you will pay.

Avoid Harbor if: you have one LLC in one state, you are comfortable filing your own $50 annual report online, or you flinch at custom quotes. You will pay more for service you do not use.

Cost check: Harbor registered agent ($99 year one, $149 after) plus a quoted annual-report filing fee plus state fees, versus doola Starter ($297/yr) with formation and EIN included. For a single-state founder, doola is usually the lower first-year total with less paperwork.

Recap checklist

  1. Budget Harbor at $149/yr per state, not $99. The intro rate lasts one year.
  2. Ask for the annual report service fee in writing before ordering, and compare it against your state's direct filing fee.
  3. Count your states. Multi-state agent fees multiply; get the full-stack quote.
  4. If you only need formation plus EIN, check doola's published pricing before requesting a custom quote anywhere.
  5. Remember change-of-agent state fees when switching providers mid-year.

FAQ

Does Harbor Compliance have hidden fees?

No per-document delivery fees, which Harbor states explicitly, and the $99-to-$149 renewal is disclosed on the registered agent page. The real complaint is that add-on services like annual report filing and foreign qualification are custom-quoted, so buyers cannot budget until they talk to sales. That is opaque pricing, not fraud, but it feels hidden at checkout.

How much is Harbor Compliance registered agent service?

$99 for the first year for new customers, renewing at $149 per year, per state, based on official pricing on harborcompliance.com as of September 2026. State change-of-agent fees are extra.

Is Harbor Compliance worth it for a single LLC?

Usually not. A single-state founder is paying enterprise-grade compliance overhead for a 15-minute annual report. A formation bundle like doola Starter ($297/yr) covers formation, EIN, and address at one published price.

How do I switch from Harbor to another registered agent?

File a change-of-agent form with your Secretary of State (fee varies by state, roughly $10 to $100+) listing the new agent's name and address, then cancel with Harbor so you are not billed at renewal. Ask the new provider to confirm the state accepted the change before the renewal date to avoid double-paying.
